## Imagine this is the last year you hire elevator operators

Proton CEO Andy Yen is pushing more AI use, as we can see in [his latest statements](https://www.404media.co/manifestos-fear-and-loser-thinking-inside-protons-ai-reckoning/).

And does it sound crazy to stop hiring SWEs this year? If you said "yes, obviously", why is the CEO of a privacy-focused company saying he will stop in 2026?

I think the push comes from a lot of FOMO and internal resistance to tools.

Imagine you are the decision maker of a company and have just seen a new tool that could multiply the productivity of your staff by an unknown amount, and they don't want it.

I feel like his push towards AI is very obvious from his position. A chainsaw is much faster than an axe. Your lumberjacks tell each other to not rely on chainsaws and go back to axes.

Yen probably feels that the only way to convince internal and external stakeholders that he is not ignoring AI is to double down on AI.

We will all see how this unfolds. Maybe Proton is doing what's right, like everyone else who is pushing AI on their SWEs, or we will see a very foreseeable fall of yet another "let's go AI first" protégé.

Klarna famously tried to make their CS team redundant with AI and, also famously, rehired them quietly.

I think we are in an unprecedented time and none of us can tell who is a lumberjack with a new chainsaw or a horse that gets replaced by a car.

Until next time.
